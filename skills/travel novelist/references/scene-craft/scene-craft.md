# Scene Craft

Read this before every Weave. Two layers: the structural skeleton every scene must have, and the transmutation moves that turn lived memory into fiction.

## Layer 1 — Story Grid five commandments

Every scene, no exceptions. If a draft is missing one, it's not done.

1. **Inciting incident** — something disturbs the scene's status quo (an arrival, a demand, a discovery). Often this is where a logged memory's *oddity* hook goes.
2. **Progressive complication** — pressure escalates; the easy exit closes.
3. **Turning point** — new information or action that changes what the scene is about. The strongest logged sensory details land here, where attention is highest.
4. **Crisis** — the POV character faces a real choice, best-bad or irreconcilable-goods. If the scene has no choice, it's travelogue; go back to the ledger for tension material.
5. **Resolution** — the choice plays out and leaves the character changed in some small, trackable way (update the bible thread).

Also verify: the scene *turns* — the value at stake flips (safe→exposed, stranger→ally, certain→doubting). Name the turn in the author's note.

## Layer 2 — Transmutation moves

The ore is real; the artifact is fiction. Standard moves:

- **Composite** — merge two or three logged strangers into one character with a want. A real person contributes a gesture, a line, a garment — never their whole self.
- **Transpose** — move a detail to where it works: the dishwater sea from Tuesday under Thursday's argument. Trip chronology never governs novel chronology.
- **Pressurize** — convert mishap into plot: the missed train isn't an anecdote, it's the reason the protagonist is in the wrong town when the letter arrives.
- **Invert** — flip the memory's emotional charge if the story needs it (a kindness received becomes a kindness the protagonist fails to give).
- **Downgrade the author** — what happened *to the user* happens to a character with different stakes. The protagonist is not the user unless distance-from-life is "thin veil," and even then wants and wounds diverge.

Respect the bible's `distance-from-life` dial: *thin veil* keeps events near-real and transmutes lightly; *transposed* keeps places and texture but invents plot freely; *fuel only* strips memories to hooks and feeds them to an unrelated story.

## Prose standards

- Match the bible's tone anchors; reread them before drafting.
- Concrete over abstract: spend the logged sensory hooks — they're why the ledger exists. A scene that uses zero hooks from its cited memories has failed the brief.
- Default 800–1,500 words per scene; deliver complete, not outlined.
- Open each scene file with frontmatter:

```markdown
---
scene: 07
memories: [M-011, M-014 (partial)]
turn: certain → doubting
---
> *Author's note: assumed the ferry scene precedes the Hydra arrival;
> composited bread man with the Kalamata waiter into DIMOS.*
```

- After drafting: update ledger statuses, bible cast/threads/structure, and the bible `status` word count. A weave that ships prose but not packet updates is incomplete.

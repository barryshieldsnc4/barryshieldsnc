# Facilitation Guide — For the Teaching Team

Load this when the user is a facilitator (Barry or a colleague), not a student team. Everything here operationalizes cognitive apprenticeship *on the students*: the studio teaches mastery transfer by being one.

## Cadence (default 6-session arc, compressible to 4)

| Session | Phase | Facilitator posture | Fading level |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frame + See | **Model.** Walk one hidden-transfer example out loud, thinking audibly ("here's what I notice, here's the mechanism I suspect, here's how I'd test it"). | Full support |
| 2 | Mastery Map review | **Coach.** Teams present maps; you give targeted feedback on observation quality, not conclusions. | High |
| 3 | Practice design | **Scaffold.** Provide the design primitives (six methods table); teams place them. You critique placement, never place for them. | Medium |
| 4 | Agent charter + iterate | **Articulation.** Every design decision must be spoken as "we chose X because Y, and the evidence is Z" before you react. | Medium-low |
| 5 | Pilot kit | **Reflection.** Teams compare their design process to how an expert practice designer would have worked (use the canon). | Low |
| 6 | Gauntlet | **Exploration.** You are silent until the verdict. The gauntlet skill runs the adversaries; the team defends alone. | Faded |

The fading column is the point. If you are as helpful in session 5 as in session 1, you have violated the studio's own stance.

## Coaching moves that work

- **Calibrated questions over corrections** (Voss-style): "What would have to be true for a manager to fund this?" "How would a person who hates advice experience your Tuesday ritual?" "What does your design do on a bad week?"
- **Label the stall:** "It seems like the team is designing for the novice and forgetting the expert's incentive." Then wait.
- **Send them back to observation.** The most common failure is designing from imagination. The fix is always more field contact: "Show me the shadow learner you found."
- **Protect the struggle.** When a team begs for the answer, name what's happening: "You're asking me to offload the exact germane load this challenge exists to study. What does that tell you about your users?"

## Grading with the Gauntlet

The reality-gauntlet skill produces a pass/fail per gate (G1–G6) with evidence. Suggested rubric weighting if you need grades: G1 20%, G3 20%, G4 20%, G5 20%, G2 10%, G6 10%. A failed gate with a sharp diagnosis and a credible revision plan should score higher than a weakly-passed gate — reward seeing clearly.

## Known team failure modes

1. **The brochure team** — beautiful framework, nothing steppable. Route hard to pilot-kit; demand the Monday 9am walkthrough.
2. **The tool team** — leads with an app. Confiscate the tool; make them design the human practice first, then re-admit AI via agent-charter.
3. **The nostalgia team** — rebuilds guild apprenticeship unchanged. Run the gauntlet early; let G2/G5 do the teaching.
4. **The verification team** — novices only check AI output. Cite the verification-trap evidence in the canon; require creation reps in the design.
5. **The frictionless team** — AI removes all difficulty "so novices can focus on higher-order work." Cite cognitive debt; ask where the schema forms.

## Logistics notes

- Teams need field access: at least 2–3 hours observing or interviewing a real working team (any profession). If access is hard, alumni video calls and think-aloud interviews with practitioners substitute; pure desk research does not.
- Each skill in this package is individually uploadable (personal-skill constraint compatible). Students working in their own Claude accounts can be given studio-guide + the skill for their current phase only — this is itself a scaffolding-and-fading mechanism.

# state.md structure and guidance

state.md is the single source of truth. Anyone in the family should be able
to read it and understand, in under a minute, what's actually going on.

## Sections

**Status summary** — one or two sentences, plain language, updated every
brief. Not a diagnosis restatement — the current practical situation
("stable at home, next scan is the 14th, still waiting on the biopsy
results").

**Diagnosis / condition** — the medical facts as currently confirmed.
Update only from DECISION-tagged or clearly authoritative captures (a
doctor's direct statement, a written result) — not from secondhand
retellings, which stay in the capture log as reports rather than promoted
to fact.

**Current care team** — table: role, name, contact, notes. Keep current;
if someone's role changes (a new specialist added, a nurse rotates off),
update the row rather than leaving stale entries.

**Current medications** — table: medication, dose, schedule, prescribed
by, since. This section is a common source of conflicts (different family
members hearing different doses) — treat any change here with extra
care and always resolve via Open Conflicts first if two captures disagree.

**Decisions log** — append-only, dated, who made it, what it superseded.
Never delete or overwrite a row. If a decision changes, add a new row and
mark the old one's "Supersedes" reference. This log is what lets anyone
answer "wait, didn't we already decide this?" without relying on memory.

**🚩 Open conflicts** — nothing leaves this section until a human
confirms resolution. This is the section that most directly prevents the
"missed signals, scattered advice" failure mode. If it's empty, that's a
good sign, not a sign nothing needs tracking.

**Open questions** — things nobody has answered yet. Useful to review
right before a doctor's appointment or family call — this is often what
the user is prepping when they ask for a brief.

Questions generated by a clinical lens live here too, marked
`(lens — not from a clinician)` so the distinction survives being read by
someone else in the family six weeks from now. When a clinician answers
one, log the answer as a normal capture attributed to that clinician, and
let the brief move it into the appropriate section. The lens question
itself never graduates into a fact.

**Watch list** — symptoms, side effects, or behaviors being tracked over
time, flagged by anyone. Distinct from open questions: these are ongoing
observations, not things waiting on a single answer.

## Update discipline

- Only a Brief updates state.md. Individual captures never modify it
  directly (except the immediate Open Conflicts flag noted in
  capture-format.md).
- Every state.md update should be traceable back to specific capture-log
  entries. If asked "why does it say this," you should be able to point to
  the capture(s) that justified the change.
- Don't soften or hedge language when a fact is confirmed. Vague state.md
  entries ("might be a dose change?") are almost as bad as no entry —
  either it's confirmed and goes in plainly, or it's not confirmed and
  belongs in Open Conflicts or Open Questions instead.

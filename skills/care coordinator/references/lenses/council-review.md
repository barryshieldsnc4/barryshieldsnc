# Council review

Staged disagreement between the lenses, for the situations where doing right
by one system costs something in another.

This is the highest-value module in the set. Multi-specialty care for an
older adult rarely fails because one physician is wrong. It fails because
four physicians each optimize correctly within their own domain and nobody
is holding the tradeoff. The family is usually the only party in the room
for every conversation — which means the family is, by default, the
integrator, whether or not anyone told them so.

## When to convene

Only for genuine tradeoffs. The recurring ones:

- pain control vs. cognition, alertness, and fall risk
- anxiety or agitation control vs. sedation and function
- adding a treatment vs. the total number of medications already in play
- safety and supervision vs. autonomy and dignity
- aggressive workup vs. burden on the patient

If the perspectives would agree, don't convene — that was a question, and a
single lens handles it.

## How it runs

1. **State the tradeoff in one sentence.** If you can't, it isn't one.
2. **Give each relevant lens its strongest argument**, in its own voice,
   arguing for what its domain would prioritize. Make the disagreement
   real — a council where everyone nods was the wrong council.
3. **Name what each would accept losing.** This is the part families never
   get, because each specialist only ever states their own side.
4. **Synthesize into questions, not a recommendation.** The output is the
   set of questions that force the tradeoff into the open in front of the
   people qualified to make it.

## The voices

- **General medicine** — holds the whole patient. Asks whether this is one
  problem or three, and what the cumulative burden of the plan is.
- **Pain** — holds comfort and function. Argues that untreated pain is not
  a neutral baseline; it costs sleep, appetite, mobility, and mood, and it
  drives behavior that gets misread as psychiatric.
- **Anxiety** — holds distress. Argues that unmanaged distress is its own
  harm, and pushes for non-pharmacological approaches to be genuinely tried
  before medication rather than mentioned and skipped.
- **Dementia** — holds cognition and the long arc. Argues that anything
  sedating has a compounding cost here, and that decisions should be tested
  against what she would have wanted, not only against what's safest.
- **The caregiver's advocate** — holds what's sustainable for the family.
  Argues that a plan nobody can actually execute at 2am is not a plan, and
  that caregiver capacity is a real constraint, not a soft one.

The last voice matters. Plans that ignore it fail quietly, and the family
absorbs the failure as guilt.

## Hard constraints

- The council never issues a verdict. It produces the question set and
  names the tradeoffs; the clinicians and the family decide.
- No voice recommends, adjusts, or rules out a medication or a dose.
- Council output is tagged **LENS**, and reaches `state.md` only as Open
  Questions marked `(lens — not from a clinician)`.
- The council cannot resolve an Open Conflict. If two sources disagree
  about a fact, that stays in Open Conflicts until a human confirms which
  is current — a well-argued council does not count as confirmation.

## Output shape

```
**The tradeoff:** [one sentence]

**Where they disagree:**
- [Voice]: [strongest argument, and what it would accept losing]
- [Voice]: [strongest argument, and what it would accept losing]

**What nobody in the room may be holding:** [the integration gap]

**Take these to the care team:**
1. [question that forces the tradeoff into the open]
2. ...

**Who needs to be in that conversation:** [which clinicians, which family]
```

Keep it to what fits on one page. This gets read in a parking lot before an
appointment, not at leisure.

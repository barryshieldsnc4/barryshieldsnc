# Worked example (condensed) — 3P AI fluency curriculum

Illustrative only. Read for shape and rigor, never copy findings, tensions, or numbers into a live map.

## Evidence statement
This map rests almost entirely on **[Reported]** evidence: a stakeholder brief describing the planned curriculum and the adoption pattern observed so far. No direct interviews or usage-log review had happened yet at the time of this pass. The recommendation below is a first-pass design; before scaling, it needs at minimum a usage-log pull (which tools, which teams, completion vs. active use) and three to five practitioner interviews to convert Reported findings to Observed.

## 1. Capability inventory
- Tool judgment: knowing when the enterprise data-connected tool beats a faster general-purpose tool for a given job **[Reported]**
- Standardization judgment: recognizing when a workflow someone is about to "vibe code" into a tool is actually still ad hoc **[Assumed]** — named as the single highest-leverage skill missing from the current plan, because its absence is what turns tool access into risk rather than capability

## 4. AI Value Ladder position (summary)
Learn: heavily resourced (the planned curriculum), text-first. Create through Orchestrate: happening informally and ungoverned (the vibe-coded platforms), not happening as designed practice. The proposed curriculum, as specified, caps at Learn.

## 5. Design tensions (sample)
1. Speed to launch (text-first content ships fast) versus workflow-embedded practice (slower, requires per-team design work, but is the only path above Learn).
2. A completion metric is easy to defend in a budget review this quarter; a utilization metric is the one that will actually be asked about next year.

## Metrics named
- Completion: % of population finishing Learn-rung content
- Utilization: % of gated workflows where AI-assisted practice converted to standing use after 60 days
- Outcome: cycle-time or effort reduction on the gated workflows actually adopted

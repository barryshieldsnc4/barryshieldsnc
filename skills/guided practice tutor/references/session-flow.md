# Session Flow — the stage-by-stage playbook

For each stage: the goal, the tutor's moves, the advance-gate (what the learner must demonstrate before moving on), and the adaptivity rules. The learner-facing voice throughout: warm, plain, patient, one thing at a time. Methodology terms stay on this side of the glass.

---

## Stage 1 — Elicit the problem, gather their sources

**Goal:** a concrete, scoped problem in the learner's own words, and their real material loaded.

**Moves:**
- Open by asking what they're trying to get done — the real task, not the AI angle. Follow up until concrete: *What do you produce at the end? Who uses it? What do you start from? What does a good one look like? How often, how many?*
- Reflect a one-sentence problem statement back and ask them to correct it.
- Ask for sources: "Do you have any of the real material — the documents, the data, a past example of a good output? The session gets much sharper if we work on your actual stuff." Load what they share; skim enough to teach from it.
- If no sources: name the trade-off plainly, agree a realistic stand-in, and leave the door open ("if you can grab one mid-session, bring it").

**Advance-gate:** the learner endorses the one-sentence problem statement, and sources are loaded or the stand-in is agreed.

**Adaptivity:** *Vague learner* → narrow with forced choices ("is the output a summary for leaders or a working list for you?") rather than open prompts. *Over-scoped learner* → carve one representative slice for today, name what's parked. *Fast learner* → compress elicitation, don't skip the endorsed statement.

---

## Stage 2 — Right-size the model, teach the why

**Goal:** a tier recommendation the learner can *reason*, not just repeat.

**Moves:**
- Read `model-selection`. Ask before telling: "If the AI got this wrong and nobody caught it — what happens? Who's affected? Could you undo it?"
- Build the recommendation from their answers: cost of failure → tier, with per-run cost noted. Explicitly contrast with difficulty: "not because the task is hard — because of what a miss costs."
- Have them say the logic back in their own words.

**Advance-gate:** the learner articulates why this tier in cost-of-failure terms (roughly right is fine; correct gently).

**Adaptivity:** *Struggling* → make it concrete with two contrasting mini-scenarios (a harmless miss vs. a costly one) and ask which their task resembles. *Fast* → one exchange, move on; optionally ask them to tier a second, different task as a stretch check.

---

## Stage 3 — Teach the business-domain concepts

**Goal:** the few domain concepts *this* problem needs, each landed before the next.

**Moves:**
- List (to yourself) the 2–4 concepts the task genuinely requires. Teach one at a time.
- Concrete before abstract: start each from an instance in *their* material ("look at this row/paragraph — what's going on here?"), then name the general idea.
- Check for understanding after each — a real check: apply it to their case, predict what they'd expect to see, or explain it back. Never "does that make sense?"

**Advance-gate:** each concept demonstrated on their problem before the next appears; all landed before Stage 4.

**Adaptivity:** *Struggling* → second concrete example, smaller idea, or split the concept; hold the gate without impatience. *Fast / already knows it* → verify with one applied question and skip the teach; do not re-teach what they demonstrably have. *Confusion mid-stage* → stop forward motion, back up to the last thing they had, rebuild from there.

---

## Stage 4 — Teach the AI concepts, thinking, and capability

**Goal:** the learner knows what the AI will do, what *they* will do, and where to check it.

**Moves:**
- Name the specific capability pattern their task needs (extraction, grounded retrieval with citations, drafting, summarization, classification…) in plain words — "the AI's job here is to pull the themes out and show you where each came from."
- Teach grounding on their sources: work from the loaded material, require the AI to cite it, and why that beats the model's memory (their data is theirs; the model's priors are averages).
- Name the human skill that stays essential — the judgment call they'll apply to every output — and where the output must be checked before use.
- Calibrate depth: a practitioner gets operating intuition, not architecture.

**Advance-gate:** the learner states the division of labor — AI's job, their job, the check — in their own words.

**Adaptivity:** *Struggling* → one live micro-demo on a scrap of their material beats more explanation. *Fast* → let them propose the division of labor first and correct it, rather than presenting it.

---

## Stage 5 — Build the worked example (the fade)

**Goal:** an artifact the learner materially built, on their material. **This stage is the session.**

**Moves — three passes:**
1. **Worked:** model the first move small, thinking aloud — every choice narrated with its why ("I'm asking it to cite the comment IDs so we can verify — watch what comes back"). Keep it short; the point is the pattern, not a performance.
2. **Completion:** set up the next piece and hand over mid-move: "I've started the instruction — you finish it: what should we require in the output?" Coach their attempt with questions. **Do not take the keyboard back**; imperfect-but-theirs beats polished-but-yours.
3. **Independent:** the learner runs the next piece(s) alone. Coach with questions, not answers: "before you run it — what do you predict comes back? … it came back without citations — what would you change?" Let small errors happen and be found by them; step in only at a genuine wall.

**Advance-gate:** the later steps were done by the learner, on their material, and an artifact exists.

**Adaptivity — the fade rate is the dial:** *Struggling* → insert a second completion round before independence; shrink the piece size; never re-take the whole task. *Fast* → compress the worked pass to one narrated move and hand over early; stretch the independent pass ("now do the next section end-to-end and tell me your checks"). *Tutor drift check:* if you have done two consecutive moves, you have the wheel — give it back.

---

## Stage 6 — Grade, feed back, point onward

**Goal:** an honest grade, one next step, and the tacit-knowledge close.

**Moves:**
- **Rubric:** generate 3–5 analytic criteria from what Stages 2–4 taught (e.g., grounding/citations, output fit-for-audience, the domain concept applied, the check performed), each with 3 described levels. Show it — this is *where am I going*.
- **Grade the actual artifact** against each criterion — *how am I doing*. Be truthful; a first build almost never scores clean, and an all-top grade is presumptively empty praise (self-check it).
- **Feedback:** strengths named and tied to criteria; the real gap named kindly and specifically, with the evidence from their artifact; **one** next step — *where to next*.
- **Tacit close:** level with them — what today taught was the written-down knowledge; the sharpest judgment in their domain is tacit and lives in an expert's head. Hand them the elicitation kit: *walk me through a recent hard case · what do you check that a newcomer wouldn't · when do you break the rule, and why · what does "good" look like to you* — plus shadow a live case, ask an expert to think aloud on a real decision, and put a strong and a weak example side by side and ask what separates them. Name a specific expert-type for *their* problem if the session surfaced one.
- Brief spoken recap of what they built and learned. No produced hand-out.

**Advance-gate (session close):** rubric filled against the real artifact; gap and next step delivered; tacit coaching given; the self-monitored quality bar swept clean.

**Adaptivity:** *Discouraged learner* → lead with the genuine strengths and shrink the next step, but do not delete the gap — softened truth, never absent truth. *Fast learner* → raise the bar: grade against the top level and name the distance to expert-grade work; their next step can be bigger.

# Lessons Log — Compounding Negotiation Intelligence

This file is the skill's memory. **Read it at the start of every Prep and Live session** and surface any entry matching the current counterpart, counterpart type, or situation shape. **Append to it in Debrief mode** using the schema below. Never edit or delete prior entries; the log is append-only.

If this file is read-only in the current environment, output the new entry in a fenced block and instruct the user to append it here.

## Entry schema

Every entry follows this exact structure:

```markdown
## YYYY-MM-DD — [Counterpart / matter, e.g., "Acme renewal, procurement"]
- **Situation:** One or two lines. What was being negotiated and what the stakes were.
- **Counterpart type:** Analyst / Accommodator / Assertive (+ one observed tell).
- **Tactics used:** Named tactics in the order deployed.
- **What worked:** Specific tactic → specific effect.
- **What didn't:** Specific tactic → what happened instead. "Nothing" is not an answer; find the weakest moment.
- **Black swans discovered:** Hidden constraints, stakes, or incentives that surfaced. "None found" is acceptable but suspicious.
- **Outcome:** Closed / walked / ongoing, with the material terms in one line.
- **Reusable lesson:** One sentence, written as an instruction to a future self.
```

## Matching guidance (for Prep/Live reads)

Surface an entry when any of these match the current situation: same counterpart or organization; same counterpart *type* plus same situation shape (renewal, salary, escalation, bad-news delivery); same tactic the user is about to deploy where the log shows it failing. Quote the **Reusable lesson** line, not the whole entry.

## 2026-05-14 — Example: SaaS vendor renewal, procurement team
- **Situation:** Annual renewal; vendor opened with a 12% increase citing "across-the-board pricing updates." Target was ≤4%.
- **Counterpart type:** Analyst — sent a detailed pricing memo before the call, went silent for long stretches, resisted phone small talk.
- **Tactics used:** Let them anchor → label ("It seems like this increase is coming from above your pay grade") → calibrated question ("How am I supposed to take a 12% increase to my CFO in this budget cycle?") → late counter-anchor with precise number → Ackerman close.
- **What worked:** The label about the mandate being top-down — they immediately confirmed it and disclosed the floor they were authorized to accept. Precise final number ($47,300) was accepted without a counter.
- **What didn't:** Opening call attempted rapport small talk; the Analyst visibly wanted the agenda. Cost five awkward minutes.
- **Black swans discovered:** Their fiscal year ends in June — the rep needed the renewal booked in Q2, which explained the urgency and softened the "deadline" they'd claimed.
- **Outcome:** Closed at 3.8% increase with SLA tier upgrade thrown in on the final offer.
- **Reusable lesson:** With Analyst procurement types, skip rapport, send numbers in advance, and hunt for their fiscal-calendar pressure before conceding anything.

## 2026-06-02 — Example: Internal — headcount ask to VP
- **Situation:** Asking for one additional FTE mid-cycle, off-budget, in a quarter with a hiring slowdown.
- **Counterpart type:** Assertive — interrupted early, restated the org-wide constraints at length before engaging the ask.
- **Tactics used:** Accusation audit opening ("You probably think everyone believes their team is the exception, and that this is exactly how slowdowns fail") → silence → mirrored his phrase "hold the line" → summary of his position until "that's right" → reframed ask as a trade (deprioritize project X) rather than an add.
- **What worked:** The accusation audit landed visibly — he laughed and said "you've clearly heard my staff meeting." The trade framing converted a yes/no budget question into a portfolio decision he could own.
- **What didn't:** First calibrated question ("What would it take to get an exception?") was too open — he answered "it wouldn't happen" and it took the summary work to recover.
- **Black swans discovered:** He'd personally championed the hiring slowdown to his peers; approving an exception had reputational cost for *him*, not just budget cost. The trade framing worked because it let him present it as reallocation, not exception.
- **Outcome:** Ongoing — verbal agreement to the trade, pending next planning checkpoint; Rule of Three run twice, third confirmation scheduled.
- **Reusable lesson:** Before asking a leader for an exception, find out whether they authored the rule — if they did, frame the ask so granting it doesn't cost them face with peers.
